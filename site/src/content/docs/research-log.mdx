## The loop

The development log counts <Claim id="prereg.cycles" /> cycles at the time the paper draft was written. In its final
form a cycle is five steps:

1. **Find a losing cell.** Compare the current composite with the field on development instances, cell by cell: one
   function, one dimension, one budget.
2. **Trace the mechanism.** Run traces show which engine stalls or wastes the budget in that cell, and why. If the loss
   is a known property of a component (for example the (1+1)-CMA-ES on f6, f7, f11 and f13), the cell is routed to
   another component and the component is not tuned.
3. **Preregister a fix.** Before any run, a document states the probe and engine, the slice where they should help, a
   numeric range for the gain, the cells where a loss is accepted (named trades), and the acceptance rule.
4. **Screen** the candidate on development instances with that fixed rule.
5. **Confirm once** on bbob instances never used before, against the method without the change. Keep it only if the
   preregistered prediction held. Those instances are then spent.

The last step of the whole project is a frozen final test, run once. It has been run, and its two analyses (v1 and v2) are
described in [the final test FT1](#the-final-test-ft1) below. The loop leaves an audit trail for every rule: a
motivating function, a written prediction and a confirmation on unseen instances. It can say no, and it makes the
design effort countable.

## The development in stages

The acceptance rule changed over time, and each change is dated in the log. Batch numbers and rules are in
`docs/research/batches/PROTOCOL.md` and `INDEX.md`.

- **Batches 1 to 10, rule v0.** Two audit rounds on 2026-10-17 built a hybrid from the original ATM sampler and
  compared it with pycma on one harness. A change was kept if its target slice improved, the primary interval was above
  zero and no function group lost more than .02. A first holdout on fresh ids confirmed the final candidate of that
  round.
- **Batches 11 to 19, rule v1.** A/A runs (the same configuration twice with shifted seeds) showed that the guardrail
  "no group loses more than .02" failed <Claim id="calib.guardrail_null" /> of the time with no true effect. It
  was replaced by declared cells and a Holm-corrected harm test.
- **Predictions with ranges.** Round 2 of 2026-10-17 preregistered numeric ranges and not only signs; its predictions
  held <Claim id="round2.held" /> times, and the misses were informative. Ranges have been preregistered since.
- **The SLSQP result.** The first comparison with restarted SciPy SLSQP was predicted to be a lead of
  <Claim id="conf.slsqp_pred" /> for c23. It was a deficit: SLSQP led by <Claim id="conf.slsqp_actual" /> on the smooth
  functions. Every quasi-Newton route of AMM was added after that, and SLSQP stays in the field.
- **Batches 20 to 53, rules v2 to v4.** The goal became every cell and not the average: first against pycma-IPOP cell
  by cell, then (from Batch 48) against the per-cell best of pycma and SLSQP.
- **Batch 54 on, rule v5.** An arm is accepted on its development slice if no Holm harm appears, no dimension-by-budget
  interval lies below zero, a two-level shrinkage gate flags no cell outside the named trades, and the sign of the
  primary prediction holds. Fresh-id confirmations remain the deciding step. Components such as the kink route, the pair
  test and the COBYQA gate were added one at a time in this period and confirmed on fresh ids. The twelve confirmations
  from c32 to c43 are summarised below.
- **Batches 75 to 93 (to the freeze).** From Batch 75 each batch has its own folder with `prereg.md` and `results.md`.
  The composites c44, c45 and c46 were confirmed on new ids. A last candidate, c47, was not.

## Predictions that held and predictions that failed

In the last twelve confirmations (c32 to c43), <Claim id="prereg.held" /> written predictions held and
<Claim id="prereg.failed" /> did not (counts as in the paper's Appendix C). These are add-one results: each component was tested on its target slice against its predecessor. They show robustness to new
instances of the same 24 functions. They do not show generalisation to other functions, and they do not measure a
component's marginal value in the final configuration.

`amm_optimizer.presets.get("c46").config_hash()` returns <Claim id="freeze.config_hash" />. The freeze is also a git tag in the
development history, which is in the research archive (not in the public repository).

The hash was <Claim id="data.c46_hash_run" /> when the in-sample runs were made. It changed at the freeze because the
configuration schema gained an opt-in field. The config text of the runs equals the config text of the frozen configuration, and the
characterisation snapshots pin the behaviour bit for bit.

The freeze note says what may still change: the benchmark harness (to run the COCO-format final test), documentation,
the paper and analysis scripts. The algorithm may not. The last confirmation that could still have changed the
composite (c47) failed on the day of the freeze.

Not in the frozen composite: the rejected or parked batches above, a held candidate, and one idea that was not screened.
The leave-one-route-out ablation was not run before the freeze. It is planned as a labelled post-freeze analysis on fresh
ids, with no pruning of the frozen configuration: <Claim id="ablation.result" />. Its preregistration was awaiting
approval when this page was written.

## The final test FT1

The final test ran the frozen configuration (<Claim id="freeze.preset" />, config hash
<Claim id="freeze.config_hash" />) once on bbob instance ids <Claim id="ids.final" />, which no design decision used.
It has two analyses of the same runs. **Version v1** (2026-10-22) included NGOpt at D 2 only, because NGOpt's runs at D 5 to
40 were still going. **Version v2** (2026-10-23) includes NGOpt at every dimension, after all 33 arms had finished, and is
the final test result used across this site (NGOpt at <Claim id="ft1.ngopt_dims" />).

### What was preregistered

The entry was written and approved by the author on 2026-10-22, before any run, with the analysis fixed in advance
(`paper/PREREG_final_test.md` on the branch `research-paper`; its FT1 entry is copied into the batch folder
`docs/research/batches/b095_final_ft1/prereg.md`).

- **Question.** Does the frozen configuration keep its development lead over the classical field on instances that no
  design decision used?
- **Data.** bbob functions 1 to 24, dimensions 2, 5, 10, 20 and 40, budgets of 10, 30, 100, 300 and 1000 evaluations per
  dimension, instance ids <Claim id="ids.final" />, one run per function, dimension, instance and budget, seed 9000:
  <Claim id="ft1.runs" /> runs of the frozen configuration in <Claim id="ft1.cells" /> cells. Metric: the share of 51
  log-spaced targets (10^2 down to 10^-8) reached within the budget.
- **Competitors**, same harness, same ids, same seed: pycma IPOP and BIPOP, restarted SciPy SLSQP, SciPy differential
  evolution, Optuna's TPE (the capped slice), Nevergrad's NGOpt; HCMA as COCO archive levels on its own instances, D 2 to
  20, labelled as such.
- **F1.** AMM minus IPOP per budget: the mean over the 120 (function, dimension) cells with equal weight per function,
  a 95% bootstrap interval over cells, Holm correction over the five budgets.
- **F2.** Cells credibly behind: some competitor leads by more than 0.02 with posterior probability above 0.9 over the
  ten paired runs (the field ledger's rule); counted per dimension and per budget, and every such cell listed by name.
- **F3.** Cells credibly ahead of every competitor that ran the cell: the same rule, flipped.
- **F4.** AMM minus restarted SLSQP per budget, as F1.
- **Falsifiers.** F1 fails if any budget's Holm-adjusted interval reaches 0. F2 above 100 cells, or F3 below 50, would
  mean the development ledger was optimistic beyond the stated ranges, and the paper would then lead with the losses.
- **Caveat written in advance.** Cell-level intervals are about 25% too narrow (lesson 42), so F2 and F3 are reported as
  counts and never as significance.

### What happened, in order

Times are 2026-10-22 up to the v1 analysis. Each deviation was logged before any FT1 result was read.

1. The field's arms started first, from about 16:15. Their results were not read until the runs of the frozen
   configuration were complete.
2. **Deviation 1, about 17:00.** The plan was to start the frozen configuration's runs after the freeze time (19:48).
   The author asked for everything to run at once, and they started at about 17:00. The freeze, the configuration, the
   data and the analysis did not change. No field result had been read.
3. **Analysis-script additions, about 18:30.** The script gained options to point it at other data and to write JSON,
   and three descriptive tables (cells where AMM has the highest mean, AMM minus IPOP per dimension and budget, AMM minus
   HCMA's level). They are not part of F1 to F4. A dry run on the development data reproduced that data's ledger.
4. **NGOpt.** At about 18:00 the decision was to let NGOpt run in full and wait. **Deviation 2, about 18:20,** replaced
   it: publish a first version without NGOpt at D 5, 10, 20 and 40, and add them in a second version. F1 and F4 do not
   involve NGOpt. For F2 and F3 the preregistration notes the known effect, from development data only: with fewer
   competitors per cell F2 can only fall and F3 can only rise.
5. **v1.** The analysis ran at 19:27, when every arm except NGOpt at D 5 to 40 had finished
   (`final_ft1.py --ngopt-dims=2`). Its output and the JSON with the numbers are the record of v1.
6. **v2, 2026-10-23.** After all 33 arms had finished, the same preregistered script, `final_ft1.py`, was run with no
   arguments, so that NGOpt counts at every dimension. The script was not changed between the two analyses; the flag in
   step 5 is the only difference. The output (`ft1_output_v2.txt`) and the JSON (`ft1_v2.json`) are the record of v2, and
   the site reads them. F1, F4 and the HCMA comparison are identical in v1 and v2, because they do not involve NGOpt.

## Lessons the log kept

The log has a dated list of lessons. Four that changed the protocol:

- Run the strongest simple baseline on the same harness first. Restarted SLSQP beat an early composite.
- Calibrate every guardrail with an A/A run before relying on it.
- Spend runs on new instance ids, not on new seeds. Only ids widen the claim.
- Use a holdout once. A holdout that has decided anything becomes development data.
