:::note[The final test]
The first part of this page is the preregistered final test **FT1**: the frozen configuration on bbob
instances <Claim id="ids.final" />, which no design decision used. The frozen configuration was run once; the
analysis was run twice, as logged in advance (first without NGOpt at D 5 to 40, which was still running, then
with every competitor at every dimension). The numbers here are from the second. The second part is development data on instances used while building
AMM, shown for context and labelled in-sample.
:::

## How to read this page

Each figure answers one question and states the conditions it holds under: budgets (evaluations per
dimension), dimensions, instance ids, seeds, competitor versions and the commit. The curves and the
cell map follow the COCO convention: the measure is the fraction of (function, target) pairs reached,
with 51 targets from 10² down to 10⁻⁸, functions weighted equally. Dimensions are never pooled, because
dimension is a fact of your problem that you can use to choose an algorithm.

### 2. The preregistered comparisons: AMM against IPOP and against SLSQP

Before any final-test run, the comparisons and the ranges they should fall in were written down. Each number
is the mean over 120 (function, dimension) cells of AMM's share of targets minus the competitor's, with a 95%
bootstrap interval over cells (2,000 resamples) and a Holm correction over the five budgets. Every budget is
above zero for both comparisons.

| Budget | AMM minus IPOP (F1) | Predicted | AMM minus SLSQP (F4) | Predicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 x D | <Claim id="ft1.f1_10" /> | +.06 to +.17 | <Claim id="ft1.f4_10" /> | −.01 to +.03 |
| 30 x D | <Claim id="ft1.f1_30" /> | +.12 to +.25 | <Claim id="ft1.f4_30" /> | .00 to +.05 |
| 100 x D | <Claim id="ft1.f1_100" /> | +.20 to +.36 | <Claim id="ft1.f4_100" /> | +.05 to +.15 |
| 300 x D | <Claim id="ft1.f1_300" /> | +.20 to +.36 | <Claim id="ft1.f4_300" /> | +.13 to +.27 |
| 1000 x D | <Claim id="ft1.f1_1000" /> | +.12 to +.27 | <Claim id="ft1.f4_1000" /> | +.20 to +.35 |

All ten results fell inside the predicted ranges, and so did the two cell counts below: every preregistered
prediction of the final test held. The lead over SLSQP is small at small budgets, where a
quasi-Newton method is hard to beat on smooth functions, and grows with the budget.

### 3. Cell by cell: where AMM leads and where it trails

Each cell is one function at one dimension and budget. Blue means AMM leads the best competitor in that cell
by more than 0.02; orange means it trails by more than 0.02, with the leader named. Cells within 0.02 are
pale. This is a descriptive plotting rule on the mean over the ten instances, not a significance test, and it is
**not** the "credibly behind" rule used for the counts below, so the map shows more orange cells than the
<Claim id="ft1.f2_behind" /> that the preregistered rule counts.

## What to know about this data

- **Final test:** one seed per (function, dimension, instance, budget), ten instances per cell. HCMA is archive
  data on other instances and exists for D 2 to 20 only; TPE ran a capped slice of the grid; NGOpt ran at every
  dimension. Where a competitor is missing (HCMA at D 40, most of TPE), the best competitor is the best of the
  others, so those cells are easier to win than a full comparison would be.
- **Development data:** one seed per instance, ten instances. The AMM series is a composite: 528 of the 600
  cells come from the full-grid run of the previous configuration c45, and 72 cells (D 10, 20 and 40 at
  100 x D) from the literal frozen configuration c46, which differs from c45 by one parameter from D = 10.
- **Package versions recorded** for the development runs: Python 3.12.10, NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.18.1, pycma
  4.5.0, cocoex 2.8.2. The Optuna and Nevergrad versions and the hardware were not recorded for these runs.
