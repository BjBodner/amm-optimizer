## Abstract

The best classical optimizer for a continuous black-box problem depends on the budget and on the landscape, but existing
routers decide from the dimension and budget alone, from which engine improved last, or from features fitted to one
benchmark. AMM routes a known budget among quasi-Newton, trust-region, coordinate, multistart and evolution-strategy
engines with online probes of curvature, plateaus, separability, kinks, basins and fractal structure. Each probe has an
engine mapping, a default outcome and a dimension-by-budget validity range, and its evaluations count against the
budget.

AMM was built on bbob in a loop that diagnoses a losing cell, preregisters a fix and confirms it on never-used instances
(<Claim id="prereg.held" /> predictions held). One configuration was then frozen and tested once on the official
instances <Claim id="ids.final" /> and beyond bbob (<Claim id="beyond.bbob" />). During development, an intermediate
configuration led IPOP-CMA-ES on never-used instances by <Claim id="conf.c32_ipop" /> in target share. In the final
test, AMM's mean target share exceeds IPOP-CMA-ES's by <Claim id="ft1.lead_ipop" /> (budgets of 10 to
1000 evaluations per dimension; Holm-corrected, bootstrap intervals above zero). AMM has the highest mean
target share, ties included, in <Claim id="hero.highest" /> of <Claim id="ft1.cells" /> function, dimension and budget
cells (<Claim id="hero.highest_pct" />). It is credibly behind some competitor in <Claim id="ft1.f2_behind" /> cells,
all listed, and credibly ahead of every competitor that ran the cell in <Claim id="ft1.f3_ahead_all" />. Against HCMA's COCO archive levels (D 2 to 20, other instances) AMM is ahead from
30 evaluations per dimension: <Claim id="ft1.hcma_30" /> at 30 x D, <Claim id="ft1.hcma_100" /> at 100,
<Claim id="ft1.hcma_300" /> at 300 and <Claim id="ft1.hcma_1000" /> at 1000 x D; the margin at 10 x D is small
(<Claim id="ft1.hcma_10" />). AMM is an open-source Python package, installable with `pip install amm-optimizer` (PyPI release:
<Claim id="release.pypi" />).

*Adapted from the paper draft of 2026-10-21. The final-test result is stated as the number of cells where AMM has the
highest mean target share (ties included), together with the preregistered count of credibly behind cells. The abstract on this page
was updated to the final-test numbers of this site.*

## Contributions

- **AMM**, a budget-aware router among classical algorithms driven by online probes with stated costs, defaults and
  validity ranges, each traced to the function that motivated it.
- **Held-out evidence** for one frozen configuration on a ladder of controls, from each engine alone to the per-cell
  oracle, with its losses named. Results (final test): the highest mean in <Claim id="hero.highest" /> of
  <Claim id="ft1.cells" /> cells and credibly behind in <Claim id="ft1.f2_behind" />; the [limits page](/limits/) lists
  those cells.
- **The development loop** as a method in its own right, with its record: <Claim id="prereg.held" /> preregistered
  predictions held in the last twelve confirmations (see the [research log](/research-log/)).
- **An open-source Python package**, installable with pip, and COCO data for every budget multiplier. Release:
  <Claim id="release.pypi" />.
