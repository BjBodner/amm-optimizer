```python
import numpy as np
import amm_optimizer

def rosenbrock(x):
    return float(np.sum(100 * (x[1:] - x[:-1] ** 2) ** 2 + (1 - x[:-1]) ** 2))

result = amm_optimizer.minimize(
    rosenbrock, x0=np.zeros(5), bounds=(-5, 5), budget=2000, seed=0,
    config=amm_optimizer.presets.get("c46"),
)
print(result.nfev, result.fun)
print(result.x.round(3))
```

```text
2000 2.4585372370019567e-13
[1. 1. 1. 1. 1.]
```

The known optimum is x = (1, 1, 1, 1, 1) with f = 0. `config=` selects AMM (the frozen configuration
`c46`); without it `minimize` runs the original ATM sampler, which reaches only f = 1.7 on this example.
That output was produced by the frozen configuration `c46` (config hash <Claim id="freeze.config_hash" />) on 2026-10-22. Source and issues: <Claim id="repo.public_url" />.

## How it compares

On bbob instances that no design decision used (ids <Claim id="ids.final" />), AMM has the **highest
mean share of targets reached in <Claim id="hero.highest" /> of <Claim id="hero.cells" /> function,
dimension and budget cells** (<Claim id="hero.highest_pct" />, ties included). It is **credibly behind**
some competitor in <Claim id="hero.behind" /> (<Claim id="hero.behind_pct" />), and all
<Claim id="hero.behind" /> are listed on the [limits page](/limits/). It leads pycma IPOP-CMA-ES by <Claim id="ft1.lead_ipop" />, and restarted SLSQP at every
budget too, by <Claim id="ft1.f4_10" /> at 10 times the dimension, growing to <Claim id="ft1.f4_1000" /> at 1000
times the dimension. The field is
pycma IPOP and BIPOP, SLSQP, differential evolution, Optuna's TPE, Nevergrad's NGOpt and HCMA. The measure is
the share of targets reached, 0 to 1.

This is the preregistered final test. The frozen configuration was run once on these instances. The
analysis was run twice, as logged in advance: a first version without NGOpt at D 5 to 40 (still running
then), and this one with every competitor at every dimension. "Credibly" is the rule fixed in advance: a competitor leads by more
than 0.02 with posterior probability above 0.9 over the ten paired runs. The intervals behind it are about
25% too narrow, so these are counts and not significance statements. Every preregistered prediction for
the final test held; the [research log](/research-log/#the-final-test-ft1) has them and the history of the
analysis.

<aside class="amm-when-not">
  <strong>When not to use AMM</strong>
  It was tested on noiseless, continuous, box-bounded problems with 2 to 40 variables and 10 to 1000
  evaluations per variable. If you can get gradients cheaply, a gradient-based method is the better
  tool. Noisy objectives, discrete variables and problems outside that range are untested, and
  results beyond bbob are <Claim id="beyond.bbob" />. The <a href="/limits/">limits page</a> lists the
  cells where AMM loses.
</aside>
