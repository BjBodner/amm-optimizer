This page says what AMM does not do well, what it was and was not tested on, and what the project does not claim. It is
meant to be read before the [results](/results/), not after.

## 1. In the final test (ids 101-110)

The frozen configuration (preset <Claim id="freeze.preset" />, config hash
<Claim id="freeze.config_hash" />) was run once on bbob instance ids <Claim id="ids.final" />: 24 functions, dimensions
2, 5, 10, 20 and 40, budgets of 10, 30, 100, 300 and 1000 evaluations per dimension, ten instances per cell, one run
per cell and instance. That is <Claim id="ft1.cells" /> cells and <Claim id="ft1.runs" /> runs of AMM. The field is
pycma IPOP and BIPOP, restarted SLSQP, SciPy differential evolution, Optuna's TPE (a capped slice of the grid),
Nevergrad's NGOpt and HCMA as COCO archive levels (its own instances, D 2 to 20 only, labelled as such).

### Credibly behind: the rule

A cell counts as **credibly behind** if some competitor leads AMM by more than 0.02 in the share of targets reached
with posterior probability above 0.9 over the ten paired runs. This is the preregistered rule F2, taken from the
project's field ledger. It is not the plotting rule of the development section below. Cell-level intervals of this kind
were found to be about 25% too narrow in earlier work, so the counts are counts and not significance statements.

<Stat id="ft1.f2_behind" label="cells where AMM is credibly behind some competitor, of 600 (final test)" />

That is <Claim id="hero.behind_pct" /> of the cells. In <Claim id="ft1.f3_ahead_all" /> cells AMM is credibly ahead of
every competitor that ran the cell, and in <Claim id="hero.highest" /> cells ("<Claim id="hero.highest_pct" />") it has
the highest mean share of targets, ties included. The three counts answer different questions and overlap only in part:
a cell can be neither credibly behind nor credibly ahead.

### What was predicted

The count of behind cells and the count of cells ahead of all were preregistered as ranges before any run. The
falsifiers were also written down: more than 100 behind cells, or fewer than 50 ahead of all, would have meant that the
development ledger was optimistic beyond the stated ranges.

| Quantity | Predicted (preregistered) | Final test | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cells credibly behind some competitor (F2) | 30 to 75 of 600 | <Claim id="ft1.f2_behind" /> | Inside the range. Falsifier (above 100) not triggered. |
| Cells credibly ahead of every competitor that ran the cell (F3) | 70 to 120 of 600 | <Claim id="ft1.f3_ahead_all" /> | Inside the range. Falsifier (below 50) not triggered. |

Both counts are inside their ranges, and so are the ten comparisons with IPOP and SLSQP (F1, F4), which are on the
[research log](/research-log/). Every preregistered prediction of the final test held. The research log records how the analysis was run and
the deviations from the plan.

The final test confirms the direction of the development result and does not prove that AMM wins on other problems: it
is still the same 24 functions, on new instances. See "What is not claimed" below.

## 2. Development context, in-sample ids 61-70

:::caution[Development data, not the final test]
Everything in this section is **in-sample**: bbob instance ids <Claim id="ids.dev" /> were used while AMM was being
built, and steered about twenty composites. It uses the plotting rule below on one seed per instance, not the
preregistered "credibly behind" rule of section 1. Do not compare the two counts.
:::

In the in-sample comparison of the frozen configuration (preset <Claim id="freeze.preset" />) against pycma IPOP and
BIPOP, SLSQP, differential evolution, Optuna's TPE, Nevergrad's NGOpt and HCMA, there are
<Claim id="hero.cells" /> cells, each one function at one dimension and one budget. The rule is fixed:

- **ahead** if AMM's share of targets reached exceeds the best competitor's by more than 0.02: <Claim id="field.ahead" /> cells;
- **level** if the difference is within 0.02: <Claim id="field.level" /> cells;
- **behind** if the best competitor leads by more than 0.02: <Claim id="field.behind" /> cells.

One seed per instance and ten instances per cell: there is no interval, so 0.02 is a plotting threshold and not a
significance test. This is not the preregistered "credibly behind" count of section 1, which uses a posterior
probability over the paired runs; the two counts are different and are not mixed here. The same cells appear on the
[results](/results/) heatmap under this plotting rule.

### The pattern in the behind cells

The tables below list every behind cell. Reading them, in order of how clearly the data show it:

- **Restarted SLSQP is the most frequent leader.** It leads where AMM is behind more often than any other
  competitor, mostly at 10 to 100 evaluations per dimension, mainly on the unimodal functions with moderate or high
  conditioning (f8 to f13) and on Gallagher's f21 and f22. The paper's development record found the same pattern early,
  when SLSQP beat an early composite on smooth functions. This table does not establish the cause.
- **Small budgets lose more cells than large ones.** More behind cells sit at 10 and 30 times the dimension than at
  300 and 1000. Nevergrad's NGOpt leads mostly at 10 and 30 times the dimension; IPOP, BIPOP and differential
  evolution lead mostly at 100 times the dimension and above.
- **Dimension 2 has the most behind cells**, and D = 40 the fewest.
- **Function groups.** Few separable-function cells (f1 to f5) are behind. The rest are spread over the moderate- and
  high-conditioning groups, the adequately structured multimodal group and the weakly structured multimodal group. The
  functions with the most behind cells are f8 and f9 (Rosenbrock, unrotated and rotated), then f7 (step ellipsoid), and
  f10, f12, f13, f17 and f21.
- **HCMA's leads are against archive data.** HCMA is not run in this harness: its values are the COCO archive's, on
  other instances, and cover D 2 to 20 only. A cell where HCMA leads says that AMM is below an archive level, not that
  HCMA beats AMM in a paired run.
- **The paper's own section on losses describes an earlier configuration.** The section "Where AMM does not win" in
  the paper draft was written for composite c43. Its largest gaps (f7 at D = 5 and 100 times the dimension, against
  IPOP; f9 at D = 10 and 300 times the dimension, against SLSQP) have changed since: the f9 cell is unchanged and
  still listed below, while the f7 cell is much smaller. The list in section 1 is the one to use.

## 3. Problem classes the paper flags as risks

These come from the paper's discussion of limitations. The final test measures bbob only, so none of them has been
measured on it.

- **Rotated separable problems.** Probes that read coordinate structure (the pair test, the coordinate-search route,
  STEP restarts) cannot fire on a rotated separable function. AMM falls back to its general path and has paid for the probe.
- **Anytime use.** AMM chooses its route from the budget, so a run stopped early returns the incumbent of a schedule
  planned for the full budget. An anytime measure can rank AMM below its final-budget value. COCO's anytime ECDF
  represents it only with one dataset per budget.
- **Cheap objectives.** AMM is written in Python. Overhead per evaluation matters when the objective is fast and the
  dimension is small. The only stored figure is for SciPy's COBYQA alone, <Claim id="limits.cobyqa_ms" /> per evaluation
  at D 2 to 3, measured outside COCO's procedure. The figure for AMM as a whole is <Claim id="timing.per_eval" />.
- **Probes outside their calibrated range.** In development, a separability route calibrated at 300 to 1000
  evaluations per dimension misrouted rugged functions at 3000, with a loss of <Claim id="limits.sep_misroute" /> per
  cell, and a fractal exemption lost <Claim id="limits.f16_loss" /> on f16. Validity ranges limit such failures; they
  cannot rule out a misreading on a problem class unlike bbob's.
- **Absolute constants.** The kink threshold, the restart tolerance and the finite-difference step are absolute, and the
  probe scales are fixed fractions of the box width. Several probes are invariant to a positive affine map of f; the constants are not.

## 4. Intended use and out of scope

A model-card summary. "Tested" means exercised in the benchmark runs behind this site; "untested" means nothing was run
and no claim is made either way.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Intended use** | Minimising a noiseless, continuous objective in a box, with 2 to 40 variables, when the number of evaluations is known before the run and lies between 10 and 1000 times the number of variables. |
| **Needs** | Exact function values (finite differences and ties rely on them), a box (bounds on every variable), a start point and a budget, and a seed for reproducibility. |
| **Tested on** | The 24 noiseless bbob functions, five dimensions (2, 5, 10, 20, 40), five budgets (10, 30, 100, 300, 1000 times the dimension). The final test (ids <Claim id="ids.final" />) covers the same grid. The paper's plan also covers 3 variables and 3000 times the dimension; those results are pending. |
| **Not an anytime method** | The route depends on the budget. Stopping a run early gives the incumbent of a plan made for the full budget. |
| **Better tools exist when** | Gradients are cheap (use a gradient-based method), the budget is a handful of evaluations per variable with a smooth function (a restarted quasi-Newton method leads in some of the cells above), or each evaluation is so cheap that Python overhead dominates. |
| **Out of scope, untested** | Noisy objectives, discrete or mixed-integer variables, constraints other than the box, multi-objective problems, more than 40 variables, budgets below 10 or above 1000 times the dimension (3000 pending), objectives that fail or return infinities as a rule. |
| **Beyond bbob** | <Claim id="beyond.bbob" />. MA-BBOB: <Claim id="mabbob.result" />. Transformed bbob and real problems are not yet tested. |

## 6. How many variants and cycles were tried

A rule set built on 24 functions invites the objection that it is a decision tree fitted to them. The design effort is
therefore counted, not hidden.

- **Cycles.** <Claim id="prereg.cycles" /> development cycles were logged when the paper draft was written, in
  <Claim id="prereg.batches" /> numbered batches, with <Claim id="prereg.confirmations" /> holdout or fresh-id
  confirmation runs and <Claim id="prereg.composites" /> promoted composites. The batch index has grown since: it
  reaches cycle <Claim id="prereg.last_cycle" /> at the freeze. See the [research log](/research-log/) for the loop, the failed predictions and the freeze.
- **Arms screened and CPU hours.** <Claim id="effort.arms_cpu" />. These are to be reconstructed from the arm files and run logs.
- **Spent instance ids.** Every confirmation used a block of bbob instance ids that no earlier decision had used, and
  that block is then spent. At the freeze: spent <Claim id="ids.spent" />, reserved and never used
  <Claim id="ids.reserved" />, next free <Claim id="ids.next_free" />. The ledger is a file
  (`docs/research/batches/spent_ids.json`) in the research archive (DOI: <Claim id="archive.doi" />, pending), not in the public code repository. The analysis code checks it before any claim is scored.
- **Rejected and parked.** The log keeps rejected candidates, including some that passed the screen and failed the confirmation. [The research log](/research-log/) tabulates them.

## 7. How the in-sample numbers differ from FT1

| | Development field (in-sample, section 2) | Final test FT1 (section 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Instances | bbob ids <Claim id="ids.dev" /> | bbob ids <Claim id="ids.final" />, never used in any decision |
| Use of the data | compared with the field after each composite; steered about twenty composites | run once on the frozen configuration |
| Runs | one seed per instance, no interval | one run per cell and instance, seed 9000; the means over cells carry bootstrap intervals (F1, F4) |
| Cell rule | ahead, level or behind by 0.02 on the mean, a plotting threshold | credibly behind if some competitor leads by more than 0.02 with posterior probability above 0.9 over the paired runs; credibly ahead against every competitor that ran the cell |
| Configuration | a composite of c45 and c46 runs (section 8) | the frozen configuration itself |
| Competitors | uneven coverage (section 8) | the same field; HCMA at D 2 to 20, TPE on a capped slice |
| Behind cells | <Claim id="field.behind" /> of <Claim id="hero.cells" /> under the 0.02 rule | <Claim id="ft1.f2_behind" /> of <Claim id="ft1.cells" /> under the credible rule |

The two behind counts answer different questions, because the rules differ. Neither is a forecast of the other, and a
difference between them is not a measure of how much the development numbers overstated the result. The comparison that
tests the development result is the one against the preregistered ranges in section 1 and on the
[research log](/research-log/).

## 8. Caveats about the development data

1. **The c46 series is a composite.** <Claim id="data.c46_from_c45" /> of the <Claim id="hero.cells" /> cells come from
   full-grid runs of the previous configuration c45. Only <Claim id="data.c46_literal" /> cells (D 10, 20 and 40 at 100
   times the dimension) were run with the literal c46 configuration, which differs from c45 by one parameter from D = 10.
   The other cells are assumed identical and were not rerun. The frozen configuration's hash is
   <Claim id="freeze.config_hash" />; at the time of the in-sample runs it was <Claim id="data.c46_hash_run" />. The
   config text is the same; the hash changed because the configuration schema gained an opt-in field.
2. **Uneven competitor coverage.** HCMA has no D = 40 values. TPE ran a capped slice only. NGOpt has no D = 10 runs (the final test has NGOpt at every dimension). Where
   a competitor is missing, the best competitor is the best of the others, so those cells are easier to win than a
   seven-way comparison suggests. The list above names the missing competitors in each cell.
3. **Run records.** Most run files were produced in development worktrees with uncommitted changes, and the Optuna and
   Nevergrad versions and the hardware were not recorded. See the [reproduce page](/reproduce/).
4. **Seed variance is not measured.** One seed per instance and ten instances give no estimate of run-to-run spread:
   <Claim id="variance.seed" />.

## 9. A no-free-lunch note

No optimizer outperforms another when performance is averaged over all possible objective functions (Wolpert and
Macready, 1997, "No free lunch theorems for optimization"). AMM's results are therefore claims about a class of
problems, not about optimization in general. It uses structure that bbob functions have and that a random function
does not: a known budget, locally smooth or separable or multi-basin structure that cheap probes can detect, and
exact evaluations. Each probe has a default outcome so that a misreading costs evaluations and not the run, but a
misreading is still possible on a class unlike bbob's, and the 24 functions it was developed on are also the 24 it was
confirmed on, in new instances only. Whether the probes read mechanisms and not function identities is the question
the problems beyond bbob are meant to test, and it is open: <Claim id="beyond.bbob" />.
